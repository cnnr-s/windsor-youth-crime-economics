# Hard Times, Hard Crimes: Youth Police Contact and Local Economic Conditions in Windsor-Essex

> Is police-reported youth (12–17) involvement in violent, property and drug offences correlated with local economic conditions across Windsor-Essex municipalities, *2021-2025*?

### Importance Of This Question
Every issue is best solved by tackling the root cause. If youth police contact rises and falls with local economic conditions, that points to where support for young people might do the most good: the communities where opportunity and economic prosperity is thinnest.

### Approach
This project analyzes eight Windsor-Essex County municipalities: Windsor, LaSalle, Tecumseh, Lakeshore, Essex, Kingsville, Leamington and Amherstburg.

- **Outcome:** youth (12–17) involvement with police (youth charged plus youth not charged), per 1,000 youth, grouped into violent, property, drug and other offences.
- **Economic indicators:** unemployment rate and low-income rate.
- **Years:** the census years 2001, 2006, 2011, 2016 and 2021, matching annual municipal crime data to census population and economic data.
- **Method:** testing for a relationship using correlation and regression analysis. Results show correlation, not causation.

Note: assumptions will be made throughout this research (for example, estimating Amherstburg's 2021 numbers after Windsor Police took over its policing in 2019). Feel free to flag any gaps in logic or assumptions that weaken the validity of the research to the writer, Connor Symons.

### A Personal Note
Over the last 10 weeks I've been volunteering for Youth Diversion, a non-profit organization specializing in providing young adults with the proper guidance and resources to become better leaders in our society.

My role involved participating in their community program as a leader / mentor. I've come to find out many participants in the program are participating due to judicial orders under the Youth Criminal Justice Act (YCJA). 10 weeks, 2.5 hours a week, 25 total hours is the difference between a clean record and a charge. This story is shared by a lot of the young men and women involved in similar programs like this across the country. In the data, many of these youth appear as "youth not charged", which is why this project counts them alongside those who were charged.

I got to know a lot of these kids, hear their story, listen to their dreams. I saw them on good days and bad, saw them frustrated, saw them happy and laughing, saw them existing and interacting with the world. There wasn't a single person that rubbed me the wrong way throughout its entirety.

They say it takes a village to raise a child. Every week I had the same nagging feeling that somewhere along the way, the village failed.

I don't look at these kids and see criminals. I don't look at these kids and see future criminals either. I see a group of people searching for importance, belonging, love, acceptance, and guidance - and aren't we all?

My goal with this research is to get a better understanding of the economic foundation our society relies on, and whether it really serves to benefit some of those who need structure the most.
